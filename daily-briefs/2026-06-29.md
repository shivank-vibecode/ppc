Broken down by campaign:

| Deriv-App-Promo-June | 506 | 27.1% | 9.9% | 0.40% | 0.40% | 0.40% |

The Audience Network sub-campaign is the worst offender: 3,814 installs, zero first trades, and only 0.8% Real Account rate. These users overwhelmingly bounce before even creating an account.

The Deriv-App-Promo campaign is the outlier — 5x better FTT rate than the main campaign. Small sample (506 installs), but the funnel shape is qualitatively different: higher signup, higher Real Account, and meaningfully better FTD/FTT. This suggests the targeting or creative for this campaign is reaching a more relevant audience.

These are small-volume campaigns, but their quality is genuine: users who arrive through brand keywords or product-specific searches (DBot, TradingView, login) are far more likely to trade.

### Attribution data integrity issue on Google's FTDs campaign [HIGH]

Google's `row-prosp-cpa-all-app-0526-en-android_FTDs` campaign reports 178 installs but 31,974 total attributions and 363 FTTs. An FTT rate of 204% is impossible — the conversion events in the "Unique users" columns are clearly not scoped to the install count. This makes Google's aggregate FTT rate (18.59%) unreliable as a single number. The individual campaign rates (3-8%) for other campaigns are trustworthy because their attribution-to-install ratios are reasonable.

Two campaigns with significant volume produced zero first trades:

The FB Audience Network campaign is straightforward waste — low engagement across every funnel step. The Google Multi Asset campaign is more interesting: 45.8% signup rate and 16.0% Real Account rate but zero FTDs and zero FTTs. These users are engaging but not converting to money — possibly because the "multi asset" messaging attracts users interested in learning/demo, not live trading.

## What Would Sharpen This

### Facebook cost efficiency — currently UNKNOWN confidence
- **Data needed**: Daily campaign-level spend from Facebook Ads Manager for June 1-29. CSV with columns: Date, Campaign Name, Amount Spent (USD).
- **Where to get it**: Facebook Ads Manager > Ads Reporting > Breakdown by Day + Campaign
- **What it unlocks**: Cost-per-FTT calculation. If FB CPI is $0.02, then cost-per-FTT = $0.02 / 0.0008 = $25. If it's $0.50, cost-per-FTT = $625. The budget decision is entirely different in each case.
- **Impact**: Moves the "should we scale back FB?" recommendation from MEDIUM to HIGH confidence.

### Google budget headroom — currently UNKNOWN confidence
- **Data needed**: Daily campaign-level spend from Google Ads for the same period. CSV with columns: Date, Campaign Name, Cost (USD).
- **Where to get it**: Google Ads > Reports > Campaign-level daily report
- **What it unlocks**: Whether high-FTT Google campaigns are budget-capped. If `row-prosp-cpc-all-login` produces 7.5% FTT but only spends $20/day, there may be room to scale it significantly.
- **Impact**: Enables a concrete budget reallocation recommendation with expected FTT impact.

### Attribution model clarification — needed for data trust
- **Data needed**: Confirmation of the AppsFlyer attribution window settings for the FTDs campaign. Are the "Unique users" columns scoped to click-through installs only, or do they include view-through and re-attribution?
- **Where to get it**: AppsFlyer dashboard > Attribution Settings, or ask the AppsFlyer admin
- **What it unlocks**: Determines whether Google's aggregate quality numbers are trustworthy or inflated. Currently treating individual campaign rates (3-8%) as reliable and the aggregate (18.59%) as unreliable.

### Retention by source — currently LOW confidence on DAU estimates
- **Data needed**: Day 1, 7, and 30 retention rates by media source from AppsFlyer Cohort Report, for install cohorts in June.
- **Where to get it**: AppsFlyer > Cohort Analysis > Group by Media Source
- **What it unlocks**: FTT is a one-time event. A source could produce high FTT but users who trade once and never return. Retention data would tell us which source actually produces habitual traders (true DAU), not just one-time converters.

### Organic baseline — currently no visibility
- **Data needed**: Total daily installs from Google Play Console for June 1-29 (organic + paid combined). CSV with columns: Date, Total Installs.
- **Where to get it**: Google Play Console > Statistics > Installs > Daily
- **What it unlocks**: When FB scaled from 60% to 98.5% of paid installs, did total installs (including organic) grow proportionally? Or did organic decline — suggesting FB is cannibalizing users who would have installed for free?

No amount of optimization will fix this — the placement is attracting users with no intent to trade. This is a HIGH-confidence recommendation; the data is unambiguous. 3. **Before any other budget decisions, get spend data.** The single most important missing data point is cost. Every recommendation beyond pausing the clearly wasteful AN campaign requires understanding cost-per-FTT. Provide daily campaign-level spend from both Facebook and Google for the last 30 days.
